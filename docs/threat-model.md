# Threat model

Patch3Q is built for the Q-Day attacker: someone who holds every classical (ECDSA) key and can record all
ciphertext today in order to decrypt it later. It is also built for the everyday attacker: a vendor that ignores a
report, a mailbox that leaks, or a disclosure platform that doxxes researchers.

## Protected

- **Report confidentiality.** The report is a QoreSeal ML-KEM-1024 envelope for the maintainer and every bound auditor. ECDSA
  keys cannot decrypt it.
- **Researcher anonymity (anonymous mode).** No reporter account exists on-chain. A relayer anchors the case, and the
  reporter is known only by `keccak256` of a fresh per-case ML-DSA-87 public key.
- **Payout unlinkability.** The payout address lives inside the ciphertext. The chain stores
  `keccak256(abi.encode(PAYOUT_COMMITMENT_TYPEHASH, caseIdHash, recipient, salt))` and learns the address only when
  the bounty is released. A fresh wallet per case is generated by default.
- **Size privacy.** Plaintext is padded to 16 KiB, 64 KiB, 256 KiB or 1 MiB before encryption, so the envelope
  length reveals only a bucket. The bucket is authenticated, and a mismatch fails verification.
- **Rationale privacy.** The severity rationale is committed as `keccak256(salt ‖ rationale)`, so common phrases cannot
  be confirmed by guessing.
- **Payout authorization.** V4 requires the program plus threshold-many unique auditor ML-DSA-87 signatures; after
  escalation the reporter replaces the program. Duplicate, unauthorized or rotated auditors do not count. A stolen
  ECDSA key, one sub-threshold auditor group, or the relayer cannot release funds.
- **Availability.** V4 publishes the exact encrypted bundle bytes in event logs and verifies their SHA-256 on recovery.
- **Payout integrity.** The signed digest includes the payout recipient, and the contract checks it against the sealed
  commitment. Redirecting the payout invalidates both the signatures and the commitment.
- **Replay protection** across cases, registry deployments and chains.
- **Accountability.** Deadlines are public. Missing one lets anyone move the case to `Escalated`, and the program
  permanently loses its vote. Late acknowledgement and triage calls revert, so the program cannot race a keeper after
  expiry. Reporter + auditor approval resolves the escalated case, enabling redacted disclosure and refund of any
  escrow remainder.
- **Integrity** of the ciphertext, the authenticated metadata (roles, deadlines, privacy profile, approval
  authorities), the commitments and the case binding.
- **EVM lane.** Auditor and relayer EVM windows are scoped to the registry, so a stolen classical key cannot use them
  against other contracts.

## Public by design

Deadlines, amounts, lifecycle events, the program and auditor accounts, the relayer address, the block timing and the
fact that a case exists are all public. Hiding them would make ghosting unprovable. The final payout address becomes
public at release. Use a fresh address, which is the default.

## Residual limits

- **Funding is opt-in.** Patch3Q cannot force a program to configure or fund a pool. V4 rejects new reports if the
  pool cannot reserve the promised amount.
- **Auditor trust remains.** V4 removes a single signer as the liveness bottleneck, but a threshold can still collude,
  refuse a valid report or disappear. There is no appeal court or timeout-based automatic payout.
- **Post-triage scope.** Deployed V3/V4 enforce acknowledgement and triage only. A program that completes those steps
  can still withhold payout approval. Live V5 adds a configurable decision deadline after `triageBy` (seven days by default), including cases
  marked resolved without a payout. Late program payouts and resolution cannot race escalation.
- **Rejected reports.** Live V5 adds a separate auditor-quorum rejection verdict that returns the complete reserve
  and frees capacity. Neither program nor reporter must co-sign a rejection. A colluding auditor threshold can
  therefore reject a valid claim; an external appeal process is still needed.
- **After payout.** Generated payout wallets use ordinary ECDSA. Escrow authorization does not protect received funds
  unless the recipient separately uses a PQ-protected account or wallet. The live V4 rehearsal's recipient has no
  registered PQ key. All three paid V5 recipients were separately PQ-registered; their closed EVM windows
  rejected actual classical transfers. Owners can grant future permissions, so these checks do not guarantee
  every future recipient-wallet configuration.
- A relayer can refuse to relay or delay relaying. It cannot read, redirect or approve anything, and the researcher can
  use another relayer.
- Deadline enforcement is permissionless but not autonomous. Someone has to send the transaction. If the program never
  funded the escrow, escalation cannot create funds.
- The contract cannot decrypt the AAD. Recipients must compare the on-chain roles with the verified bundle.
- Network-level metadata, such as the IP address that delivered the bundle to the maintainer, is outside the protocol.
- Anyone who holds the per-case reporter vault can act as the reporter after escalation. Treat it like a private key.

## Out of scope

The following are out of scope:

- a compromised browser before sealing
- stolen recipient vaults
- malicious recipients
- unsafe redaction
- loss of V3/off-chain ciphertext availability (V4 publishes the bundle durably)
- failure of the chain or the precompile
- timestamp manipulation within normal block-production bounds
- bugs in unaudited dependencies, including pre-1.0 QoreSeal

`pnpm audit --prod` reports no known vulnerabilities. The high-severity `picomatch` advisory was removed at its root:
Patch3Q no longer installs wagmi, RainbowKit or WalletConnect. A direct EIP-6963/EIP-1193 adapter handles QoreX and
other injected wallets, so no glob engine or WalletConnect storage layer is present in the production dependency tree.
